# Agent tips: Owner content

Technical lessons for the coding agent in this area, each naming the project that learned it.
Read them before working here. A new lesson in this area goes to the project's *Upstream
candidates* in the moment, and the lesson harvest adds it here.

- **In an interview, people volunteer private details and then ask to keep them out.** Redact
  them in the private notes too (a placeholder plus a rule saying what it stands for and where it
  mustn't go), and search the public repo for earlier mentions (alexanderwebber.com).
- **Figures the owner volunteers from a data export still need their confirmation** before a
  story uses them: the export missed activities recorded in other apps.
- **If the owner wants to review a multi-part draft once, at the end,** keep a clean
  read-through file apart from the working notes, with the choices still open marked inline.
- **For a personal story in a public repo, tell the specify subagent what mustn't appear, then
  search its output yourself.** Keep the owner's raw answers in a private file the next session
  can find. Write the brief from the owner's exact words: "no one else in the family appears"
  became "names no one else" in a brief and reached the spec, where it would have banned
  crediting a published source. Only the agent's own read of the spec caught it.
- **An owner's screenshot can show something other than their complaint.** A screenshot sent
  about a cut-off button showed the button whole and a card's foot cut. Say what the screenshot
  shows, and ask which one they mean before changing anything.
- **An interview that builds a record (a CV, a case study) asks the business *why* behind each
  change as it goes:** what problem it solved, and for whom. Six rounds captured what and how, and
  the owner's review of the first draft found every claim without a reason. Lead with what sets
  the person apart, not what is now the norm. Ask when each responsibility began, separately from
  job titles: a layout by title read as if the person had only recently started managing people.
- **Research the public facts before an interview** (a company's history, products, terms) and
  bring them for confirmation; ask only what only the owner knows. For a career story, rough eras
  beat exact dates (alexanderwebber.com).
- **Check a framing the agent invents against the record before it's drawn.** An "eras" framing
  put a role years later than the record had it, and the owner spotted it at once. When the owner
  corrects a fact, grep every output for it: a wrong title had already reached three documents.
- **Briefs for an agent working in the owner's other systems** (an employer's tools, another
  assistant): say what is needed back (the output's headings and confidentiality rules) with an
  example prompt, and let the owner adapt it and decide what runs there. Ask for a general summary
  of named sources and say "report only what's there": headings shaped by what the agent assumes
  invite the other agent to fill gaps (alexanderwebber.com).
- **A public document gated on something private states the gate's rule, not its reason**
  ("real text waits until the owner's sharing rules are re-checked", with no word on why). Before
  committing, scan the staged diff for words that hint at the reason, not only the private-terms
  list.
